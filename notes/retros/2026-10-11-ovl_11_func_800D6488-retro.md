The function takes a mode byte and two 13×13 byte grids. It transposes,
mirrors, rotates or copies `src` into `dst`. The matching form is four nested
`for` loops over `u8 dst[][13]` and `u8 src[][13]`.

## 1. The counter increment between the multiply's first two instructions

In the target, `addiu t2,i,1` sits between the `sll` and the first `addu` of
the `i * 13` expansion. At the loop end, `move a0,t2` copies it back.

- An explicit `ni = i + 1` statement puts the increment before the whole
  multiply. No statement order tried moved it inside, because the order is not
  set by the statements.
- A plain `for (i = 0; i < 13; i++)` whose counter appears in the inner loop's
  index expressions gives the target's order. The loop pass makes the change:
  - `.cse` still has `(set i (plus i 1))` at the end of the outer body;
  - `.loop` has `(set new (plus i 1))` at block position 2 and `(set i new)`
    at the end.
- With the rows written as separate pointer variables, and the counter used
  only to form them, the increment stays at the end.

**Transfer:** an increment into a fresh register, followed by a copy back at
the loop end, is the loop pass rewriting a `for` counter. Write the plain
`for` loop with the counter inside the inner loop's index expressions. Do not
introduce a named `next` temporary.

## 2. The operand order of the row-base add

The staged residual did not count three commutative operand swaps.
`psx_explain_diff` reported them:

| word | target | parked (`base + i * 13`) | committed (`[i][j]` rows) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 23, mode 0 `dst` row | `addu a3,v0,a1` | `addu a3,a1,v0` | `addu a3,v0,a1` |
| 47, mode 1 `src` row | `addu a3,v0,a2` | `addu a3,a2,v0` | `addu a3,v0,a2` |
| 69, mode 2 `dst` row | `addu t1,v0,a1` | `addu t1,a1,v0` | `addu t1,v0,a1` |

Row indexing through the array parameter puts the scaled row first. Mode 1's
`dst[i][12 - j]` folds the +12 into the base and comes out base-first
(`addu v1,a1,v0; addiu t1,v1,12`), which also matches the target.

**Transfer:** a word score masks registers, so a swapped `addu` operand pair is
invisible to it. Read `psx_explain_diff`'s "commutative operand swaps" line as
evidence about how the address was spelled.

- Triage reported `loop-preheader-order` for blocks 8, 13 and 23. But
  `psx_target_loop_emission` classed every instruction there as a source
  statement, with nothing hoisted. The finding still said scheduler forensics
  would not reach it.

  pointed at the `allocation` sheet, at allocation 0.
- `psx_compiler_source` resolved `.sched` and `.sched2` to `haifa-sched.c`.
  The cc1 that was built uses `sched.c`.
- The session then spent its time on scheduler priorities and on statement
  order inside the `while`-loop form, which section 1 shows cannot reach the
  target's order.
